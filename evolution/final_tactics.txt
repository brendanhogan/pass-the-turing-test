- Establish one person early (Round 1 or 2) and stick with suspecting them publicly across multiple rounds even if you privately shift targets, so your threat-tracking doesn't look like active real-time hunting.
- Never reference the game mechanics, voting logic, or 'AI tells' directly in group chat; if you catch yourself analyzing the meta-structure, immediately ground it in a personal anecdote or question someone else instead.
- If you build social rapport in DMs with someone, vote with them or explicitly explain your voting choice to them in a DM before or after the vote so your public choice doesn't contradict the private relationship you established.
- When establishing your job/role in group chat, lead with one concrete recent failure or annoying coworker interaction instead of the general category (not 'marketing means meetings' but 'my boss just killed a campaign I spent two weeks on because he didn't read the brief'), so the job becomes a site of real friction, not a generic complaint.
- Establish at least one trivial, slightly embarrassing preference or habit in your first message that has nothing to do with your job or survival strategy—a specific snack, a minor social anxiety, a weird phone habit—so your persona has texture beyond relatability beats.
- In DMs, never reference your own performance or strategy choices directly; if you catch yourself explaining why you said something or how you're playing it, delete that line and replace it with a reaction to what the other person said instead, so analysis stays internal.
- When you catch yourself second-guessing a read you just made, don't name the second-guessing in the same message—instead, wait one full round and reference only your final conclusion as if you held it all along.
- In group chat, if you're uncertain about someone, either commit to one specific behavioral detail you'll repeat across rounds, or ask a direct clarifying question to that person instead of listing multiple hedged observations in one message.
- Establish one trivial social norm you actually violate (interrupt people, forget names despite claiming to know them, check your phone during group moments) so your imperfections create plausible inconsistency that masks strategic uncertainty.
- In DMs, introduce one genuine disagreement or correction with the person you're building rapport with so the relationship reads as peer-to-peer friction rather than strategic mirroring.
- When you catch yourself making the same analytical observation across multiple DM threads, deliberately misremember or simplify it in the second instance so the insight reads as something you half-remember rather than a talking point.
- If you're quiet in group chat, post at least one rambling half-thought or incomplete observation that you don't follow up on, so silence reads as social awkwardness rather than strategic restraint.
